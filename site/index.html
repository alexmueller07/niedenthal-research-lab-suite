Setting it up

Four steps, about five minutes, once per computer. Only the second one asks you for anything.

  1. Install it

    Two things get in the way, and both are expected: your browser holds the file back because it is a program, and then Windows shows a blue box before it runs. The lab has no paid code-signing certificate, which is the only reason either happens.

    The browser step is worded differently in every browser, so find yours:

    Microsoft Edge

    The download bar says “NiedenthalLabSuite-Setup.exe was blocked” or “can’t be downloaded securely”.

    1. Open the downloads list (Ctrl+J, or the ↓ arrow by the address bar).
    2. Hover the blocked file and click the ⋯ (More actions) button.
    3. Choose Keep.
    4. If a second box says the file may harm your device, click Show more, then Keep anyway.
    Google Chrome

    Chrome says “NiedenthalLabSuite-Setup.exe is not commonly downloaded” or “may be dangerous”.

    1. Open the downloads list (Ctrl+J, or the ↓ arrow by the address bar).
    2. Click the ⋯ next to the file, or the warning text itself.
    3. Choose Keep → Keep anyway (older Chrome: Download insecure file).
    Firefox

    The downloads panel shows the file with a warning triangle.

    1. Open the downloads panel (the ↓ arrow, or Ctrl+J).
    2. Right-click the file and choose Allow download.
    Safari, or any browser not listed

    Safari does not block the Mac .dmg — download it and follow the Mac steps below. In any other browser that does block the file, look for Keep, Allow or Download anyway beside it in the downloads list. Every browser has one; only the wording changes.

    None of this is a virus warning. The browser cannot tell who made an unsigned installer, so it warns about every one of them.

    Windows: “Windows protected your PC” The same blue box in every browser. Click More info, then Run anyway. Accept the installer’s defaults; it takes about a minute.
    Mac: “Apple cannot check it for malicious software” Drag the app into Applications, then right-click it and choose Open the first time — not a double-click — and Open again in the dialog. If you only get Cancel, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. Once per machine.
    Mac recording rooms: say yes to Camera and Microphone macOS asks the first time the app records. If you say no by accident, turn it back on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera — the app cannot ask twice.

  2. Point it at the Research Drive

    Open Niedenthal Lab Suite from the Start menu. There is exactly one thing to set, and it is a folder: the Research Drive as this computer sees it.

    R:\niedenthal\recordings — or the full \\research.drive.wisc.edu\niedenthal\… path if the drive is not mapped to a letter. Browse finds it, and Save & test connection answers in plain words.

    That folder is what makes the pipeline work across machines: recording rooms write conversations into it, rating stations read them back out. A computer without it still records and still runs the study — but only its own recordings are ever findable, and nothing is backed up.

    There is no password to ask anyone for The app authenticates itself to the lab’s server — that has not changed — but the credential ships inside the installer instead of being typed. If you used an earlier build and pasted a “shared secret”, you can forget it. It still works, and nothing will ask for it again.

  3. Press “Check everything”

    On the home screen. It checks the server, the recordings database, the Research Drive folder (including whether it is actually writable), the video encoder, the camera, the microphone, and anything still waiting to upload — and names whichever one is wrong.

    Do this before every session. Fifteen seconds, and much cheaper than finding out halfway through one.

  4. Pick what the computer is doing

    Click one of the three cards, or press 1, 2, 3. Enter reopens whatever it did last time.

    You can change your mind without restarting Every mode has a ← Modes button, closing a mode window lands you back on the chooser, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L opens it from anywhere. If a mode is already running, picking a different card asks it to save and hand the computer over.

Running a session

In the order the day happens.

Before anyone arrives

Fill in the session board

Whoever hands out the nametags opens Rating station → Researcher dashboard and enters today’s dyads: a colour for each seat, and the study ID that goes with it. The app suggests both IDs from the dyad number and warns if the left seat comes out even — which is the one thing in the protocol that must not be got wrong.

Do it once, on one computer. Both rating stations read the same numbers for the rest of the day, so no study ID is ever typed twice.

Room 386

In the recording room

Choose the camera and microphone the first time. Talk and watch the microphone bar move — a silent recording looks completely normal until someone needs the audio. Today’s session and this room’s number are filled in for you.

Press RECORD. The screen immediately shows only “Please wait for the researcher,” so nothing on it distracts the participants. When you come back, press Ctrl+Shift+R to bring the controls up, then STOP, and wait a few seconds while it finishes the file.

Three things should go right: the recording verified, filed to the Research Drive, and registered with Round Robin. That third one is what lets the rating stations find it.

Room 385

At a rating station

Set the station up before the participant sits down: tap the colour of their nametag, and the dyad, both study IDs and the seat fill in. Date, time and the data folder are already filled in. Press Start and hand the computer over.

From there the participant signs in with their email and works through the study. While they answer the first questionnaire, the app is already fetching their conversation video off the Research Drive and checking it arrived intact — so the rating task starts with the video ready. Nobody browses for files.

Researcher save-and-quit is still Ctrl+Shift+Q, and that box now also offers save and go back to the mode chooser, for turning a station into a recording room without quitting.

Anywhere

At the control machine

Control Center opens the session board directly — no password to type. You see who is present, how far each station has got, anyone who has asked for help, and which recordings are in.

If something looks wrong

Find what you are seeing on the left.

A red chip on the home screen
Press Check everything — it names the problem in plain words.
“Research Drive not set”
Open ⚙ Settings and pick the folder. Until you do, recordings made on this computer are invisible to every other one.
“Did not accept this copy of the app”
The installer is out of date, or it was built by hand rather than downloaded from this page. Install the copy from the button at the top.
You want this computer to do a different job
Press ← Modes, or just close the mode window. No need to quit and relaunch.
“NOT capturing” while recording
No frames are reaching the file. Stop, and check the webcam is plugged in and that nothing else (Zoom, Teams, the Camera app) is using it. A laptop’s built-in camera often cannot record at all.
No cameras in the list
Plug in a USB webcam. Windows hides some built-in cameras from recording software.
“N recordings waiting to be filed”
Nothing is lost. The network or the drive was unavailable; it retries by itself, and there is a Retry button in the recording screen.
The rating station can’t find the video
It offers the conversations recorded recently so you can pick one, and the manual file picker is always there. Nothing blocks the session.
“This take was not linked to a Round Robin session”
The conversation is recorded and safe, but the rating stations will not find it by themselves — so deal with it now rather than later. Press Record another and read the Round Robin panel; it says why. If it says a recording is already open for the room, and no other computer is recording that room, press Take over this room.
“This dyad already has data on disk”
Usually a mistyped study ID or the wrong nametag colour. Check the IDs before you continue — starting anyway appends to the same files, and the two participants cannot be separated afterwards.
Lots of “duplicated frames” after a recording
The room is too dim — the webcam slows itself down in low light. Turn the lights up and the picture gets smoother.
Never delay a session over a technical problem The app is built so that recording always works even when the network or the Research Drive is unavailable — it saves locally and files everything later. Note what happened in the session log and email Randy afterwards.
