Lab Suite

The recording rooms, the rating stations, and the session board — one app, one install.

Download for Windows

Version 0.1.0 · 83 MB · Windows 10 or 11

Install the same app on every lab computer. Each time you open it, you choose what that computer is doing right now — nothing is locked to one job.

Recording room

Records the conversation. For the room computers with the webcam.

Rating station

Runs the PPS study a participant sits down to. It finds their conversation video by itself.

Control Center

The live session board — who is present, how far along they are, which recordings are in.

Setting it up

  1. Install it

    Download the file above and double-click it. Accept the defaults; it takes about a minute.

    Windows will show a blue “Windows protected your PC” box. This is expected — the app isn’t signed with a paid certificate. Click More info, then Run anyway.

  2. Paste the shared secret — once per computer

    Open Niedenthal Lab Suite from the Start menu. It opens on Settings the first time. There is exactly one box to fill in: the lab’s shared secret. Paste it and click Save & test connection, which answers in plain words.

    Ask Alex or Randy for the shared secret. It is deliberately not written on this page — it is the one thing standing between the internet and participant names, emails and schedules. You type it once per machine and never see it again.

    Everything else is already set: the app knows where the lab’s server is, and it keeps recordings in a folder it manages itself. (An admin can later point it at the Research Drive under Advanced, which is what lets a rating station on a different computer reach a recording.)

  3. Press “Check everything”

    On the home screen. It checks the server, the secret, the recordings database, the recordings folder, the video encoder, the camera, the microphone, and anything still waiting to upload — and tells you which one is wrong if any is.

    Do this before every session. It takes about fifteen seconds and it is much cheaper than finding out mid-session.

  4. Pick what the computer is doing

    Click one of the three cards (or press 1, 2, 3). Close that window when you’re done and you land back on the chooser next time. From inside any mode, Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L brings the home screen back.

Running a session

In the recording room

Choose the camera and microphone the first time, and a folder to save to. Talk and watch the microphone bar move — a silent recording looks completely normal until someone needs the audio. Today’s session and this room’s number are filled in for you.

Press RECORD. The screen immediately shows only “Please wait for the researcher,” so nothing on it distracts the participants. When you come back, press Ctrl+Shift+R to bring the controls up, then press STOP and wait a few seconds while it finishes the file.

You should see three things go right: the recording verified, it was filed to the recordings folder, and it was registered with Round Robin. That third one is what lets the rating stations find it.

At a rating station

The participant signs in with their email and works through the study. While they answer the first questionnaire, the app is already fetching their conversation video in the background and checking it arrived intact, so the rating task starts with the video ready. Nobody browses for files.

Researcher save-and-quit is still Ctrl+Shift+Q.

At the control machine

Control Center opens the session board directly — no password to type. You see who is present, how far each station has got, anyone who has asked for help, and which recordings are in.

If something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to do
A red chip on the home screen Press Check everything — it names the problem in plain words.
“NOT capturing” while recording No frames are reaching the file. Stop, and check the webcam is plugged in and nothing else (Zoom, Teams, the Camera app) is using it. A laptop’s built-in camera often cannot record at all.
No cameras in the list Plug in a USB webcam. Windows hides some built-in cameras from recording software.
“N recordings waiting to be filed” Nothing is lost. The network or the drive was unavailable; it retries automatically, and there is a Retry button in the recording screen.
The rating station can’t find the video It will offer the conversations recorded today so you can pick one, and the manual file picker is always available. Nothing blocks the session.
Lots of “duplicated frames” after a recording The room is too dim — the webcam slows itself down in low light. Turn the lights up and the picture gets smoother.
Never delay a session over a technical problem. The app is built so that recording always works even when the network or the Research Drive is unavailable — it saves locally and files everything later. Note what happened in the session log and email Randy afterwards.

A few things worth knowing

  • Recordings are participant data under IRB 2020-1657. They belong on the Research Drive — never a personal folder, never cloud storage.
  • The app records at high quality on purpose: about 1.4 GB per 10-minute conversation. The study measures facial expression, so detail matters more than disk space.
  • The participant screens are identical to the app the lab used before. Nothing a participant sees or does has changed.
  • The macOS version is built but has not been tested on the lab Mac yet — ask before using it for a real session.
