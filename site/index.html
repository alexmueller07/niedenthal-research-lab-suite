Setting it up

  1. Install it on Windows

    Download the installer, then open it. Two things get in the way, and both are expected: your browser holds the file back because it is an .exe, and Windows itself shows a blue box before it runs. The lab has no paid code-signing certificate, which is the only reason either happens.

    The browser step is worded differently in every browser, so find yours:

    Microsoft Edge

    The download bar says “NiedenthalLabSuite-Setup.exe was blocked” or “can’t be downloaded securely”.

    1. Open the downloads list (Ctrl+J, or the ↓ arrow by the address bar).
    2. Hover the blocked file and click the … (More actions) button.
    3. Choose Keep.
    4. If a second box appears saying the file may harm your device, click Show more, then Keep anyway.
    Google Chrome

    Chrome says “NiedenthalLabSuite-Setup.exe is not commonly downloaded” or “may be dangerous”.

    1. Open the downloads list (Ctrl+J, or the ↓ arrow by the address bar).
    2. Click the … next to the file, or the warning text itself.
    3. Choose Keep → Keep anyway (older Chrome versions: Download insecure file).
    Firefox

    The downloads panel shows the file with a warning triangle.

    1. Open the downloads panel (the ↓ arrow, or Ctrl+J).
    2. Right-click the file and choose Allow download.
    Safari on a Mac, or any other browser

    Nothing extra to do — download the .dmg and follow the Mac steps below. If your browser is not listed and it blocks the file, look for a Keep, Allow or Download anyway option next to it in the downloads list. Every browser has one; only the wording changes.

    None of this is a virus warning. The browser cannot tell who made an unsigned installer, so it warns about every one of them.

    Then Windows shows a blue “Windows protected your PC” box. This one is the same in every browser. Click More info, then Run anyway. Accept the installer’s defaults; it takes about a minute.

    Install it on a Mac

    Open the downloaded .dmg and drag Niedenthal Lab Suite into Applications. Then right-click the app and choose Open the first time — not a double-click.

    macOS will say the app “cannot be opened because Apple cannot check it for malicious software.” Expected, for the same reason: the lab has no paid Apple developer certificate. Right-click the app, choose Open, then Open again in the dialog. If you only get a Cancel button, go to System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, and click Open Anyway. You only do this once.
    Mac recording rooms: say yes to Camera and Microphone. macOS asks the first time the app records. If you say no by accident, turn it back on in System Settings → Privacy & Security → Camera — the app cannot ask twice.

  2. Point it at the Research Drive — once per computer

    Open Niedenthal Lab Suite from the Start menu. There is one thing to set, and it is a folder: the Research Drive, as this computer sees it — a mapped letter like R:\niedenthal\recordings, or the full \\research.drive.wisc.edu\niedenthal\… path. Browse finds it, and Save & test connection answers in plain words.

    That folder is what makes the pipeline work across machines: recording rooms write conversations into it, and rating stations read them back out. A computer without it still records and still runs the study, but only its own recordings are ever findable.

    There is no password to ask anyone for. The app authenticates itself to the lab’s server — that has not changed — but the credential now ships inside the installer instead of being typed. If you used an earlier build and pasted a “shared secret”, you can forget it; it still works and nothing will ask for it again.

    Everything else is already set: the app knows where the lab’s server is.

  3. Press “Check everything”

    On the home screen. It checks the server, the recordings database, the Research Drive folder (including whether it is actually writable), the video encoder, the camera, the microphone, and anything still waiting to upload — and tells you which one is wrong if any is.

    Do this before every session. It takes about fifteen seconds and it is much cheaper than finding out mid-session.

  4. Pick what the computer is doing

    Click one of the three cards (or press 1, 2, 3).

    You can change your mind without restarting the app. Every mode has a ← Modes button, closing a mode window lands you straight back on this screen, and Ctrl+Alt+Shift+L opens the chooser from anywhere. If a mode is already running, picking a different card asks it to save and hand the computer over.

Running a session

In the recording room

Choose the camera and microphone the first time, and a folder to save to. Talk and watch the microphone bar move — a silent recording looks completely normal until someone needs the audio. Today’s session and this room’s number are filled in for you.

Press RECORD. The screen immediately shows only “Please wait for the researcher,” so nothing on it distracts the participants. When you come back, press Ctrl+Shift+R to bring the controls up, then press STOP and wait a few seconds while it finishes the file.

You should see three things go right: the recording verified, it was filed to the recordings folder, and it was registered with Round Robin. That third one is what lets the rating stations find it.

Before the day starts: the session board

Whoever hands out the nametags opens Rating station → Researcher dashboard and fills in today’s dyads: a colour for each seat, and the study ID that goes with it. The app suggests both IDs from the dyad number and warns if the left seat ends up even, which is the one thing in the protocol that must not be got wrong.

Do this once, on one computer, and both rating stations read the same numbers for the rest of the day.

At a rating station

The RA sets the station up before the participant sits down: tap the colour of their nametag and the dyad, both study IDs and the seat fill in. Date, time and the data folder are filled in already. Then press Start and hand the computer over.

From there the participant signs in with their email and works through the study. While they answer the first questionnaire the app is already fetching their conversation video off the Research Drive and checking it arrived intact, so the rating task starts with the video ready. Nobody browses for files.

Researcher save-and-quit is still Ctrl+Shift+Q — and that box now also offers “save and go back to the mode chooser”, for turning a station into a recording room without quitting.

At the control machine

Control Center opens the session board directly — no password to type. You see who is present, how far each station has got, anyone who has asked for help, and which recordings are in.

If something looks wrong

What you seeWhat to do
A red chip on the home screen Press Check everything — it names the problem in plain words.
“Research Drive not set” Open ⚙ Settings and pick the folder. Until you do, recordings made on this computer are invisible to every other one.
“did not accept this copy of the app” The installer is out of date, or it was built by hand rather than downloaded from this page. Install the copy from the button at the top.
You want this computer to do a different job Press ← Modes, or close the mode window. No need to quit and relaunch.
“NOT capturing” while recording No frames are reaching the file. Stop, and check the webcam is plugged in and nothing else (Zoom, Teams, the Camera app) is using it. A laptop’s built-in camera often cannot record at all.
No cameras in the list Plug in a USB webcam. Windows hides some built-in cameras from recording software.
“N recordings waiting to be filed” Nothing is lost. The network or the drive was unavailable; it retries automatically, and there is a Retry button in the recording screen.
The rating station can’t find the video It will offer the conversations recorded recently so you can pick one, and the manual file picker is always available. Nothing blocks the session.
“This take was not linked to a Round Robin session” The conversation is recorded and safe, but the rating stations will not find it by themselves — so deal with it now rather than later. Press Record another, look at the Round Robin panel, and it will say why. If it says a recording is already open for the room, and no other computer is recording that room, press Take over this room.
Lots of “duplicated frames” after a recording The room is too dim — the webcam slows itself down in low light. Turn the lights up and the picture gets smoother.
Never delay a session over a technical problem. The app is built so that recording always works even when the network or the Research Drive is unavailable — it saves locally and files everything later. Note what happened in the session log and email Randy afterwards.

A few things worth knowing

  • Recordings are participant data under IRB 2020-1657. They belong on the Research Drive — never a personal folder, never cloud storage.
  • The app records at high quality on purpose: about 1.5 GB per 10-minute conversation. The study measures facial expression, so detail matters more than disk space. The recording screen tells you how many more sessions the drive has room for.
  • The post-conversation video task changed (Randy, 2026-08-05): no average-UW-student ratings, each clip judged for yourself and for your partner in an order drawn per clip, and 1–7 scales instead of 1–100 sliders. Data files from before and after that change are not directly comparable on those measures.
  • The macOS version has not been run on the lab Mac yet. It builds, it is signed, and it installs — but nobody has recorded a real session with it. Ask Alex or Randy before using a Mac for anything that counts.
  • A Mac currently records with a different FFmpeg build (7.1) than Windows (9.0). For a rating station that makes no difference. For a recording room it does: the whole point of pinning one encoder is that every machine produces comparable video. Until those match, prefer Windows for recording.
